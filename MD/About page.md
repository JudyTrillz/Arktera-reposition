## 2. The Growth Problem

## The Wrong Fix Can Cost You More Than the Original Problem.

When growth slows, the instinct is often to do more: increase marketing spend, try another tactic, or introduce another tool.

But more activity doesn't necessarily produce better commercial results.

If inquiries aren't converting into sales, generating more prospects may only increase the number of missed opportunities. If sales are growing but margins remain weak, winning more clients could place additional pressure on an inefficient operation. And if clients don't return, continually pursuing new business can become an expensive cycle.

**The constraint may be in your offer, sales process, pricing, delivery, or retention.** Each requires a different response, and treating the wrong problem can waste resources without improving performance.

Before committing more time, money, or effort, you need to identify the bottleneck limiting your business and determine what needs to change.

**Growth starts with diagnosing the problem, not simply doing more.**

## 4. How We Think About Growth

## Revenue Doesn't Operate in Silos.

Revenue performance depends on how well the different parts of your business work together.

Marketing can attract the right prospects, but if your offer is unclear or your sales process is weak, those prospects may never become clients. Strong sales can bring in more business, but inefficient service delivery, poor pricing, or limited capacity can undermine profitability. And when the client relationship ends after the first sale, opportunities for repeat business and referrals can be lost.

That's why we look beyond individual activities to understand how your business generates demand, builds trust, converts opportunities, delivers services, retains clients, and generates referrals.

**Demand. Trust. Conversion. Delivery. Retention. Referral.**

Each stage presents opportunities to strengthen commercial performance. A weakness in one area can restrict the results achieved elsewhere, while improvements across connected functions can support stronger overall business performance.

The objective isn't to optimize every activity in isolation. It's to identify how the parts of your business interact and where improvements can make the greatest commercial difference.
